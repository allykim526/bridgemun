        New to MUN? How This Works

        1. The Chair runs the session — recognizing each speaker in turn, and calling votes. In this simulator, the Chair is whoever starts the meeting.
        2. Opening speeches — each delegation takes a turn speaking on the topic, usually opening with something like "Honorable Chair, fellow delegates…" and closing with what it wants the committee to do.
        3. Formal vs. simple English — real MUN speeches use diplomatic phrasing ("This delegation believes…"). This simulator can also read a shorter, plain-English version of the same point instead — turn that on per delegation in the setup below.
        4. Motions — a short procedural request, such as moving into voting procedure, extending speaking time, or asking another delegation a question. In this simulator the scripted AI delegates can raise motions too; the Chair decides whether to put one to a vote or rule it out of order.
        5. Who votes — every delegation in the room votes for itself. An AI delegate votes by itself, according to its country's researched position, and shows its reason. A student who has joined the meeting votes for their own country from their own screen. The Chair only ever casts their own country's vote — never anyone else's.
        6. Procedural votes — used for administrative matters, not the substance of a resolution. They move fast: every delegation votes Yes or No — there is no abstaining.
        7. Questions — a delegate may ask another delegation a direct question after its speech, and that delegation answers.
        8. Resolution vote & wrap-up — once debate on the topic is done, the Chair writes up the draft resolution and puts it to a final vote. Rules vary between committees (some require a two-thirds majority for certain matters), but unlike a procedural vote, a resolution vote almost always allows delegations to abstain as well as vote Yes or No. This simulator's resolution is always typed by a person, never generated automatically.
        9. Adjournment — the Chair closes the session once the resolution vote (or the last scheduled speaker) is done.
